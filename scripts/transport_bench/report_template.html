CPU: the daemon reports time.process_time_ns() (all threads) at the start and end of each measured window; the window opens after the workload's connections are established and closes before they are torn down (for connect the setup is the workload). "µs/round trip" and "µs/event" divide the daemon's CPU by the replies or delivered stream events in the window; lifecycle events such as status are not counted. Memory: VmRSS/VmHWM from /proc, peak reset at window start, and "peak" is the largest of the high-water mark and the before/after RSS readings (procfs RSS counters are per-thread cached estimates). Caveats

  • Same machine, loopback TLS, self-signed certificate; a real remote client adds network latency that dwarfs every number here.
  • The WSS client is the Python websockets library, not a browser. Browsers also negotiate permessage-deflate, so wss is the closer analogue; wss-nocomp isolates TLS + framing from compression.
  • The daemon's CPU during real tasks includes the agent itself (model call, persistence, printer), which is identical across transports; the differences are the transport share.
  • Flood latencies are queueing delay behind earlier events in the same burst, so they measure throughput, not idle-path latency; the paced bursts and paced tasks measure the latter.
  • Percentiles are nearest-rank over each round's samples, then the median across $rounds rounds on a shared 80-core host; p99 and max are sensitive to scheduler noise.
  • The stand-in model emits fixed 40-character chunks, not real tokens; "per chunk" figures are per streamed text_delta event.
